NLM-FAD — Nonlinear Magnification

The author's TypeScript / WebGL2 port of his 1990s focus+context toolkit. Four demos, two engines, one seam.

Focus+context displays magnify what you're looking at while keeping the rest on screen, compressed. Engine A is the classic approach: aim a closed-form lens. Engine B is FAD's contribution: say how much magnification you want, where — a field — and solve for a mesh that delivers it. The three Engine-B demos share one solver and differ only in where the field comes from.

Engine A · closed-form

Closed-form fisheye

Drag a radial lens over a textured grid. Three kernels, a bounded domain, an optional legible linear centre.
Engine B · brush

Paint a field

Paint where you want magnification and watch the mesh relax until each region's area matches what you asked for.
Engine B · moving data

Traffic over a zone

Planes cross a zone; their proximity becomes the field; the mesh follows the traffic and the planes ride the warp.
Engine B · image features

Magnify a face

68 facial landmarks outline each feature; a smooth kernel around the outline magnifies it while the periphery compresses to pay for it. Drop in your own photo.

Papers: Keahey & Robertson, IEEE InfoVis '96 and '97; Keahey, InfoVis '98. Source, math notes and citation file on GitHub. Needs WebGL2; the face demo also loads a small detector model on first run.
